title: "Wilms Tumour (Kidney Tumour) Surgery"
category: "Paediatric Oncology"
catid: "general"
summary: "Surgical treatment of Wilms tumour, the most common kidney tumour of childhood."
image: "/assets/img/img1.jpg"
overview: "Wilms tumour typically presents as a painless abdominal mass, often noticed incidentally by a parent, and is one of the most curable childhood solid tumours when managed through coordinated multidisciplinary care."
procedure_text: "Surgery generally involves removal of the affected kidney (nephrectomy) or, in select cases, a kidney-sparing partial resection, often performed after a course of chemotherapy to reduce tumour size, in line with established cooperative-group protocols."
prep: "Staging imaging and multidisciplinary planning precede surgery."
recovery: "Hospital stay and recovery depend on the extent of surgery performed; further treatment is guided by pathological findings."
complications: "Risks relate to the extent of surgery and proximity to major abdominal vessels and organs, discussed individually."
followup: "Ongoing follow-up is coordinated with the paediatric oncology team, including monitoring of remaining kidney function."
