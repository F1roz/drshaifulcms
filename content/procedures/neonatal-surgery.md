summary: "Surgical care for rare structural conditions identified in newborns, often requiring specialist multidisciplinary support."
image: "/assets/img/img1.jpg"
overview: "Neonatal surgery covers a group of uncommon conditions present from birth that affect the airway, gut, or abdominal wall — such as abnormal connections between the food pipe and windpipe, bowel obstruction, and abdominal wall defects. These conditions may occur in isolation or alongside other congenital differences, and often require a prolonged hospital stay together with lifelong follow-up involving multiple specialists."
procedure_text: "Surgical approach is individualised to the specific diagnosis, gestational age, and overall health of the newborn, and is planned in close coordination with neonatology and anaesthesia teams."
prep: "Newborns requiring surgery are cared for in a specialised neonatal unit with input from a multidisciplinary team before and after the operation."
recovery: "Recovery timelines vary considerably depending on the underlying condition and the baby's overall health, and are discussed individually with families."
complications: "Risks are specific to each condition and are discussed in detail with parents prior to surgery."
followup: "Long-term, multidisciplinary follow-up is typically required."
