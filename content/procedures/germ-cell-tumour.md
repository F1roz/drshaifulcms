summary: "Surgical treatment of germ cell tumours, which can arise in the ovaries, testes, or along the body's midline."

overview: "Germ cell tumours in children arise from the cells that normally form eggs or sperm, and can occur in the ovary or testis, or, less commonly, in midline sites such as the sacrococcygeal region, chest, or brain. Many are highly treatable with a combination of surgery and, when needed, chemotherapy."
procedure_text: "Surgical management typically involves complete excision of the tumour, tailored to its location — for example, removal of an affected ovary or testis, or resection of a sacrococcygeal tumour including the coccyx to reduce recurrence risk."
prep: "Staging investigations and tumour marker blood tests guide diagnosis and treatment planning."
recovery: "Recovery depends on tumour location and extent of surgery; further treatment is guided by pathology and marker trends."
complications: "Risks are specific to the surgical site and are discussed individually."
followup: "Follow-up includes clinical review and monitoring of relevant tumour markers."
