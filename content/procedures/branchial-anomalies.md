summary: "Surgical removal of congenital neck cysts, pits, or tracts arising from incomplete development of the branchial apparatus."
image: "/assets/img/img1.jpg"
overview: "Branchial anomalies arise when structures involved in early face and neck development do not fully regress, leaving a cyst, sinus opening, or connecting tract along the side of the neck. They may present at birth or become noticeable later, sometimes only after an infection."
procedure_text: "Complete surgical excision under general anaesthesia is the standard treatment, with the exact approach tailored to whether the anomaly is a simple cyst or a more complex tract."
prep: "Routine day-surgery or short-stay fasting guidelines apply."
recovery: "Recovery is generally straightforward, with wound care advice provided at discharge."
complications: "General surgical risks apply, including a small chance of recurrence if a tract is incompletely excised, and rare risk to nearby nerves depending on anomaly location."
followup: "Review at two to three weeks, with pathology discussion where relevant."
