title: "Ingrown Toenail Surgery"
category: "General Paediatric Surgery"
catid: "general"
summary: "Treatment for a nail edge growing into the surrounding skin, causing pain, swelling, and sometimes infection."
image: "/assets/img/img1.jpg"
overview: "Ingrown toenails often result from tight footwear or nail-cutting technique, and cause redness, tenderness, and occasionally discharge or bleeding along the nail edge. Initial management focuses on footwear changes, correct nail trimming, warm soaks, and antibiotics if infected. Surgery is reserved for recurrent or persistent cases."
procedure_text: "A minor procedure, often possible under local anaesthetic, in which a narrow strip of the nail, nail bed, and the growth centre (matrix) on the affected side is removed, preventing that portion of nail from regrowing."
prep: "Discussed individually — many cases can be managed without general anaesthesia."
recovery: "Simple dressing care over one to two weeks; most children return to normal footwear and activity quickly."
complications: "Small risk of infection or partial nail regrowth requiring a repeat procedure."
followup: "Wound check as advised, typically within two to three weeks."
order: 7
