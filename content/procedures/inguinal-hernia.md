title: "Inguinal Hernia Repair"
category: "General Paediatric Surgery"
catid: "general"
summary: "Repair of a groin bulge caused by a persistent passage between the abdomen and groin, common in infants and young children."
image: "/assets/img/img1.jpg"
overview: "An inguinal hernia occurs when abdominal contents protrude through a naturally occurring but unclosed passage in the groin (the processus vaginalis). It presents as an intermittent groin or scrotal/labial bulge, often more prominent with crying or straining. Unlike in adults, childhood inguinal hernias do not resolve on their own and require surgical repair to prevent incarceration."
procedure_text: "Repair is performed under general anaesthesia through a small groin incision. The hernia sac is identified, separated from surrounding structures, and tied off at its origin. In selected cases a laparoscopic (keyhole) technique may be used."
prep: "Standard day-surgery preparation and fasting instructions apply. Repair is usually arranged promptly after diagnosis given the risk of incarceration."
recovery: "Most children go home the same day. Mild fussiness on waking is common and settles quickly. Normal activity can generally resume within a few days, with heavier activity avoided for two weeks."
complications: "General surgical risks apply, along with a small risk of hernia recurrence and, rarely, injury to the blood vessels or vas deferens supplying the testis — this risk is slightly higher during repeat surgery. A hernia can occasionally develop later on the opposite side."
followup: "Review is arranged approximately three to four weeks after surgery."
