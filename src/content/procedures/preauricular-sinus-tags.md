<h2>Overview</h2>
      <p>A pre-auricular sinus is a small, blind-ending pit in front of the ear formed during early ear development; it does not affect hearing but can become infected. Pre-auricular skin tags are separate fleshy nodules with no connection to a pit and carry no infection risk, being purely a cosmetic consideration.</p>
      <h2>The Procedure</h2>
      <p>Asymptomatic sinuses can simply be observed. Infected sinuses are treated with antibiotics first, with surgical removal once settled. Surgery involves an elliptical incision around the opening; for sinuses, the full tract is traced down to the ear cartilage before complete removal.</p>
      <h2>Preparing for Surgery</h2>
      <p>Routine day-surgery fasting guidelines apply for surgical cases.</p>
      <h2>Recovery</h2>
      <p>Day-case surgery; dissolvable sutures and simple wound care over one to two weeks.</p>
      <h2>Possible Complications</h2>
      <p>Low risk of infection, bleeding, or incomplete removal requiring further surgery.</p>
      <h2>Follow-up</h2>
      <p>Review at two to three weeks.</p>
