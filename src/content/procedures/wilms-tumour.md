<h2>Overview</h2>
      <p>Wilms tumour typically presents as a painless abdominal mass, often noticed incidentally by a parent, and is one of the most curable childhood solid tumours when managed through coordinated multidisciplinary care.</p>
      <h2>The Procedure</h2>
      <p>Surgery generally involves removal of the affected kidney (nephrectomy) or, in select cases, a kidney-sparing partial resection, often performed after a course of chemotherapy to reduce tumour size, in line with established cooperative-group protocols.</p>
      <h2>Preparing for Surgery</h2>
      <p>Staging imaging and multidisciplinary planning precede surgery.</p>
      <h2>Recovery</h2>
      <p>Hospital stay and recovery depend on the extent of surgery performed; further treatment is guided by pathological findings.</p>
      <h2>Possible Complications</h2>
      <p>Risks relate to the extent of surgery and proximity to major abdominal vessels and organs, discussed individually.</p>
      <h2>Follow-up</h2>
      <p>Ongoing follow-up is coordinated with the paediatric oncology team, including monitoring of remaining kidney function.</p>
