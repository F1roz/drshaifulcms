<h2>Overview</h2>
      <p>Paediatric urology covers a broad range of conditions including vesicoureteric reflux (backward flow of urine toward the kidney), hypospadias (an abnormally positioned urethral opening), pelvi-ureteric junction obstruction, and other structural conditions of the kidney, bladder, and genitalia identified either before birth, in infancy, or in later childhood.</p>
      <h2>The Procedure</h2>
      <p>Treatment ranges from minimally invasive endoscopic techniques to open or laparoscopic reconstructive surgery, selected according to the specific diagnosis, its severity, and the child's age.</p>
      <h2>Preparing for Surgery</h2>
      <p>Pre-operative imaging (such as ultrasound or specialised urinary tract studies) guides planning; fasting instructions are provided before admission.</p>
      <h2>Recovery</h2>
      <p>Recovery varies by procedure; some operations are day-case, while reconstructive surgery may require a short hospital stay with catheter care.</p>
      <h2>Possible Complications</h2>
      <p>Risks vary by procedure and are discussed individually with families before surgery.</p>
      <h2>Follow-up</h2>
      <p>Follow-up typically includes clinical review and, where relevant, repeat imaging to confirm a good outcome.</p>
