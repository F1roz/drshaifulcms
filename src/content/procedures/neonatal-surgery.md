<h2>Overview</h2>
      <p>Neonatal surgery covers a group of uncommon conditions present from birth that affect the airway, gut, or abdominal wall — such as abnormal connections between the food pipe and windpipe, bowel obstruction, and abdominal wall defects. These conditions may occur in isolation or alongside other congenital differences, and often require a prolonged hospital stay together with lifelong follow-up involving multiple specialists.</p>
      <h2>The Procedure</h2>
      <p>Surgical approach is individualised to the specific diagnosis, gestational age, and overall health of the newborn, and is planned in close coordination with neonatology and anaesthesia teams.</p>
      <h2>Preparing for Surgery</h2>
      <p>Newborns requiring surgery are cared for in a specialised neonatal unit with input from a multidisciplinary team before and after the operation.</p>
      <h2>Recovery</h2>
      <p>Recovery timelines vary considerably depending on the underlying condition and the baby's overall health, and are discussed individually with families.</p>
      <h2>Possible Complications</h2>
      <p>Risks are specific to each condition and are discussed in detail with parents prior to surgery.</p>
      <h2>Follow-up</h2>
      <p>Long-term, multidisciplinary follow-up is typically required.</p>
