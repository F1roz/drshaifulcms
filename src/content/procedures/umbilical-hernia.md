<h2>Overview</h2>
      <p>An umbilical hernia is a soft bulge at the navel caused by incomplete closure of the abdominal wall opening through which the umbilical cord once passed. Most close spontaneously by around four years of age; surgery is considered for hernias that persist beyond this, are unusually large, or cause discomfort.</p>
      <h2>The Procedure</h2>
      <p>A short day-case operation under general anaesthesia, typically taking around twenty minutes, in which the defect is closed with dissolvable sutures.</p>
      <h2>Preparing for Surgery</h2>
      <p>Routine day-surgery fasting guidelines apply.</p>
      <h2>Recovery</h2>
      <p>Most children resume normal activity within a few days; heavy activity is avoided for two weeks.</p>
      <h2>Possible Complications</h2>
      <p>Low overall risk profile; a small chance of wound infection, bleeding, or recurrence.</p>
      <h2>Follow-up</h2>
      <p>Review at three to four weeks.</p>
