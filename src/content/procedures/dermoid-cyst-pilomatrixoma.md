<h2>Overview</h2>
      <p>Dermoid cysts arise from skin cells trapped beneath the surface during early development, typically appearing near the outer eyebrow or in the midline of the neck; they enlarge slowly and can become infected if injured. Pilomatrixomas are firm, benign lumps arising from hair-follicle cells, usually on the face or neck, and are occasionally mildly tender.</p>
      <h2>The Procedure</h2>
      <p>Both conditions are treated with straightforward surgical excision under general anaesthesia — the lump is removed in full through a small, carefully placed incision, closed with dissolvable sutures and skin glue. The operation generally takes around twenty minutes.</p>
      <h2>Preparing for Surgery</h2>
      <p>Routine day-surgery fasting guidelines apply.</p>
      <h2>Recovery</h2>
      <p>Day-case surgery; the glue dressing falls away naturally over one to two weeks.</p>
      <h2>Possible Complications</h2>
      <p>Low risk of infection, bleeding, or (rarely) recurrence if any cyst wall is left behind.</p>
      <h2>Follow-up</h2>
      <p>Review at three to four weeks, with discussion of pathology results if relevant.</p>
