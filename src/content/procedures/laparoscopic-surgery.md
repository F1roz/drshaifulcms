<h2>Overview</h2>
      <p>Minimal access, or laparoscopic, surgery uses small incisions, a camera, and fine instruments in place of a large traditional opening. In children this approach is used for conditions ranging from appendicitis and gallbladder disease to complex intra-abdominal procedures, offering less postoperative pain, smaller scars, and typically a faster return to normal activity.</p>
      <h2>The Procedure</h2>
      <p>Carbon dioxide gas is used to create working space within the abdomen, and the operation is completed through a small number of 3–12mm incisions using a camera and specialised instruments. Depending on the underlying condition, conversion to an open approach may occasionally be needed for safety.</p>
      <h2>Preparing for Surgery</h2>
      <p>Fasting and pre-admission instructions are provided according to the specific procedure being performed.</p>
      <h2>Recovery</h2>
      <p>Recovery is generally faster than with open surgery, though it varies by procedure; specific guidance is given for each operation.</p>
      <h2>Possible Complications</h2>
      <p>General surgical risks apply, including a small chance of conversion to open surgery, along with the specific risks of the underlying operation being performed.</p>
      <h2>Follow-up</h2>
      <p>Follow-up timing depends on the specific procedure performed.</p>
