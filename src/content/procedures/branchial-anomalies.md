<h2>Overview</h2>
      <p>Branchial anomalies arise when structures involved in early face and neck development do not fully regress, leaving a cyst, sinus opening, or connecting tract along the side of the neck. They may present at birth or become noticeable later, sometimes only after an infection.</p>
      <h2>The Procedure</h2>
      <p>Complete surgical excision under general anaesthesia is the standard treatment, with the exact approach tailored to whether the anomaly is a simple cyst or a more complex tract.</p>
      <h2>Preparing for Surgery</h2>
      <p>Routine day-surgery or short-stay fasting guidelines apply.</p>
      <h2>Recovery</h2>
      <p>Recovery is generally straightforward, with wound care advice provided at discharge.</p>
      <h2>Possible Complications</h2>
      <p>General surgical risks apply, including a small chance of recurrence if a tract is incompletely excised, and rare risk to nearby nerves depending on anomaly location.</p>
      <h2>Follow-up</h2>
      <p>Review at two to three weeks, with pathology discussion where relevant.</p>
