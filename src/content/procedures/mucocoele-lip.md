<h2>Overview</h2>
      <p>A mucocoele forms when a minor salivary gland duct in the lip is injured (often from lip-biting) and saliva collects under the surface, forming a soft bluish swelling.</p>
      <h2>The Procedure</h2>
      <p>Short day-case excision under general anaesthesia, with the affected gland tissue removed to reduce the chance of recurrence.</p>
      <h2>Preparing for Surgery</h2>
      <p>Routine day-surgery fasting guidelines apply.</p>
      <h2>Recovery</h2>
      <p>Paracetamol is usually sufficient for comfort in the first day; lip swelling and bruising are normal and settle within about a week. A normal diet can resume once comfortable, though vomiting on the day of surgery is common from the anaesthetic.</p>
      <h2>Possible Complications</h2>
      <p>Small risk of infection, requiring antibiotics if it develops; low risk of recurrence.</p>
      <h2>Follow-up</h2>
      <p>Review at two to three weeks.</p>
