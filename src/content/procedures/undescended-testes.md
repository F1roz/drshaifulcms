<h2>Overview</h2>
      <p>During normal development the testis migrates from near the kidney down into the scrotum, usually completing this journey before or shortly after birth. When descent is incomplete the testis is most often found along the groin, though occasionally it lies within the abdomen. Surgical correction (orchidopexy) is generally recommended between nine and twelve months of age to support future fertility and ease of examination.</p>
      <h2>The Procedure</h2>
      <p>A groin-located testis is usually correctable in a single operation, mobilising the testis and its blood supply and fixing it within the scrotum. A testis located higher, within the abdomen, may require a staged approach, sometimes using laparoscopy.</p>
      <h2>Preparing for Surgery</h2>
      <p>Routine day-surgery fasting guidelines apply.</p>
      <h2>Recovery</h2>
      <p>Typically day-case or overnight surgery, with a return to normal activity within a week, avoiding straddle toys and vigorous activity briefly.</p>
      <h2>Possible Complications</h2>
      <p>Bleeding or infection occurs in a small proportion of cases. Injury to the testicular blood supply is uncommon but more likely at repeat surgery, and can rarely lead to reduced testicular growth.</p>
      <h2>Follow-up</h2>
      <p>Review is arranged at four to six weeks to confirm testicular position and healing.</p>
